# Project Starting Point

## Selected project area
AI Workforce Investment Planner

## Why our team is interested in it

We are interested in this area because AI is becoming an important part of how companies redesign work and allocate workforce investment. However, adopting AI does not always mean replacing workers or cutting costs. We want to understand how firms and public agencies can use data to balance productivity, employee development, and automation when deciding where to invest in AI.

## Candidate Vision A
#### What: 
Decide which occupations should be prioritized for AI-enabled workflow redesign and employee AI training. Where can companies use AI to improve work?
#### For Whom: 
A company workforce strategy manager in a specific industry
#### Why It Matters: 
Companies are under pressure to adopt AI, but applying it broadly across all roles may waste resources or disrupt work unnecessarily. We want to understand which occupations are more promising candidates for AI-enabled workflow redesign, so companies can focus their effort where AI is more likely to complement existing tasks and create meaningful operational value.

## Candidate Vision B
#### What: 
Decide which occupations should be prioritized for AI-transition reskilling support based on AI exposure and labor-market outlook. Which workers may need more help adapting to AI-related changes?
#### For whom: 
A workforce development director at a public workforce agency
#### Why It Matters: 
AI will not affect every occupation in the same way. Some jobs may change gradually, while others may require workers to learn new skills more quickly. We want to explore where these pressures may be strongest and how public workforce programs could use data to better support workers through that transition.


## Core data source we confirmed we can access
Anthropic Economic Index, OpenAI Signals, O*NET Database, BLS Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS), and BLS Employment Projections.

Anthropic and OpenAI provide signals on how AI is being used across work activities and occupations. O*NET provides occupation-level task and work-activity information, while BLS provides employment, wage, industry, and future labor-market outlook data. Together, these sources may allow us to compare occupations from both a company workflow-redesign perspective and a public workforce-reskilling perspective.


## Three questions we still need to resolve
1. Can the datasets be reliably connected at the occupation and work-activity level?
2. How can we tell the difference between high AI exposure and a real opportunity for workflow redesign or reskilling?
3. Which indicators should matter most when prioritizing occupations for companies versus public workforce agencies?
