# Alternatives

## Current Decision-Making Process Without Our Tool

You are a workforce development director at the California Employment Development Department (EDD). You need to decide which occupations should receive priority funding for AI-related reskilling programs.

The decision may be triggered by a new workforce-development funding opportunity, changes in employer demand, evidence that AI is changing job tasks, or concerns that workers in certain occupations may need new skills.

Without the proposed AI Workforce Investment Planner, I would review several sources separately. I might use California employment and wage data, occupational projections, employer feedback, previous training-program results, and information from local workforce development boards. I would also consult labor-market analysts, training providers, employers, community organizations, and other government partners.

First, I would identify occupations with a large number of workers or major changes in employment demand. Next, I would examine wages, projected job openings, required skills, and training requirements. I would then ask employers and training providers whether AI is changing the work and whether useful training programs can realistically be offered. Finally, I would compare the occupations and recommend which ones should receive funding.

This process may be difficult because the information comes from different sources and may use different occupation codes. AI-use data may be national rather than specific to California. It may also be difficult to compare AI exposure, employment demand, training cost, regional needs, and equity considerations at the same time. Some decisions may therefore depend heavily on staff judgment and stakeholder opinions.
